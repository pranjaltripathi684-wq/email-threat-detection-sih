1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Email is the single most critical communication channel across government agencies,
banking institutions, healthcare networks, higher education, and enterprise 
environments. However, email is also the primary attack vector for:
  - Advanced Phishing & Credential Theft
  - Display-Name & Executive Impersonation (CEO Fraud)
  - Business Email Compromise (BEC) & Wire Fraud
  - Malicious Payload & Ransomware Delivery
  - Social Engineering & Human Exploitation

This platform is an enterprise-grade academic prototype engineered to conduct 
multi-layered, explainable, and cryptographically verified forensic analysis on 
suspicious email messages. It bridges the gap between basic spam filtering and 
in-depth digital forensics investigation.


2. THE CORE PROBLEM THIS PROJECT SOLVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Traditional spam filters, static blacklists, and signature-based gateways fail 
against modern email attacks due to the following structural blind spots:

a) Lookalike Domains with Valid Authentication:
   Attackers register typosquatted domains (e.g., "paypa1-update.com") and set up 
   legitimate DNS records (SPF, DKIM, and DMARC). Basic filters see passing 
   authentication and mistakenly mark the message as clean.

b) Business Email Compromise (BEC) via Compromised Accounts:
   When an adversary compromises a legitimate vendor's Microsoft 365 or Google 
   Workspace account, the email is sent from a real, high-reputation domain with 
   full cryptographic alignment. There are no malicious attachments or links—only 
   social engineering requesting modified bank wire instructions.

c) Display-Name Impersonation:
   Attackers configure an email client to display the name of an executive (e.g., 
   "Dr. Arthur Vance, CEO") while sending from a disposable public freemail 
   provider (@gmail.com, @proton.me).

d) Deceptive Links and Anchor Mismatches:
   Emails contain hyperlinks where the visible anchor text displays a trusted URL 
   (e.g., "https://www.paypal.com/login"), but the underlying HTML destination 
   points to an untrusted IP address or shortened URL.

e) Complex Multi-Hop Relay Obfuscation:
   Adversaries route messages through multiple intermediate SMTP servers, VPNs, 
   or open relays to conceal the actual origin.

f) Lack of Forensic Chain-of-Custody & Explainability:
   Most commercial security tools operate as "black boxes," giving a single score 
   without explaining WHY an email is dangerous, without cryptographic evidence 
   preservation, and without actionable SOC recommendations.


3. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & HOW IT WORKS STEP-BY-STEP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When an email enters the platform, it goes through an automated 5-stage pipeline:

STAGE 1: EVIDENCE INGESTION & CRYPTOGRAPHIC PRESERVATION
  1. The analyst uploads an RFC 5322 (.eml/.msg) file or pastes raw headers/body.
  2. The ingestion engine immediately computes the SHA-256 cryptographic hash of 
     the raw byte stream.
  3. The raw file is stored in an immutable, read-only evidence directory named by 
     its SHA-256 hash. The original data is never altered.
  4. An "Initial Evidence Acquisition" event is automatically written to the 
     immutable Chain of Custody ledger with a UTC timestamp and actor identity.

STAGE 2: MULTI-LAYER FORENSIC ENGINES
  The message is concurrently analyzed across five specialized forensic engines:

  1. Header & Relay Path Engine (header_parser.py):
     - Normalizes standard headers (From, To, Reply-To, Date, Message-ID).
     - Extracts all intermediate "Received:" headers and orders them chronologically 
       (Hop 1 = origin, Hop N = recipient gateway).
     - Classifies each IP address (Public, Private, Loopback, Link-Local, Bogon).
     - Calculates transit time deltas between hops to flag clock tampering.
     - Identifies the "Earliest Reliable Public IP" (the first public relay IP 
       recorded by a trustworthy receiving server).

  2. Authentication & Alignment Matrix (auth_analyzer.py):
     - Evaluates SPF (Sender Policy Framework) policies and IP authorization.
     - Evaluates DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) cryptographic body hashes and 
       signatures.
     - Evaluates DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and 
       Conformance) alignment in both strict and relaxed modes.
     - Detects "Authentic Lookalike Traps": Flags deceptive domains that pass SPF/
       DKIM authentication to mislead analysts.
     - Checks From vs. Reply-To domain alignment to detect redirection.

  3. Sender & Domain Typosquatting Analyzer (sender_domain_analyzer.py):
     - Calculates Levenshtein distance and Jaro-Winkler string similarity against 
       protected high-value brands (PayPal, Microsoft, Google, Chase, etc.).
     - Detects Unicode/Punycode (xn--) homoglyphs and character substitution 
       (e.g., digit '1' for letter 'l', digit '0' for letter 'o', 'rn' for 'm').
     - Detects freemail abuse (VIP display name using a public free webmail).

  4. Content NLP & Social Engineering Heuristics (content_analyzer.py):
     - Parses plain text and HTML, safely stripping scripts and tracking pixels.
     - Scans for urgency cues ("immediate action required", "account suspended").
     - Scans for fear & authority cues ("subpoena", "legal action", "termination").
     - Scans for wire transfer & payment cues ("updated bank account", "routing").
     - Scans for credential harvesting cues ("verify password", "login now").
     - Scans for confidentiality pressure ("keep this private", "do not call").
     - Detects embedded HTML <form>, <input>, and <iframe> elements.

  5. URL & Static Attachment Forensics (url_analyzer.py & attachment_analyzer.py):
     - URL Engine: Extracts all hyperlinks. Flags deceptive anchor text (visible 
       text != actual href), IP-based destinations, shorteners, and high-entropy 
       hexadecimal paths.
     - Attachment Engine (Zero-Execution): Reads binary magic bytes (PE "MZ" 
       executables, ELF, OLE2, ZIP), detects double extensions (e.g. 
       "document.pdf.exe"), and inspects document macros (AutoOpen, VBA scripts) 
       without ever executing the file on the host machine.

STAGE 3: INFRASTRUCTURE & THREAT INTELLIGENCE ENRICHMENT
  - Geolocation: Resolves the earliest public IP to its estimated country, city, 
    latitude, longitude, Autonomous System Number (ASN), and ISP organization.
  - Threat Feeds: Queries local and external intelligence feeds (AbuseIPDB, 
    VirusTotal, URLhaus) for known malicious IP, domain, URL, or hash matches.

STAGE 4: EXPLAINABLE 7-TIER RISK SCORING & MACHINE LEARNING
  - The Risk Engine computes a composite score (0 to 100 points) weighted across 
    7 distinct signal tiers:
      1. Header Anomalies (0 - 20 pts)
      2. Authentication & Alignment (0 - 20 pts)
      3. Sender & Domain Impersonation (0 - 15 pts)
      4. Content & Social Engineering (0 - 15 pts)
      5. URL Threat Signals (0 - 15 pts)
      6. Attachment Static Risk (0 - 10 pts)
      7. Threat Intelligence & Reputation (0 - 5 pts)
  - Risk Classification Tiers:
      * 0 - 24 pts   : Low Risk / Benign
      * 25 - 49 pts  : Suspicious
      * 50 - 74 pts  : High Risk
      * 75 - 100 pts : Critical Threat
  - Explainability: Produces an itemized list of Positive Risk Indicators, 
    Mitigating Factors, and a Prescribed Operational Action for the SOC team.
  - Machine Learning: A calibrated TF-IDF + Logistic Regression model classifies 
    the email category (Phishing, BEC, Impersonation, Financial Fraud, Malware 
    Delivery, Legitimate) and outputs the top contributing n-gram tokens.

STAGE 5: GRAPH CORRELATION, CASES & FORENSIC REPORTING
  - NetworkX Correlation Engine: Builds a network graph connecting Emails, Senders, 
    Domains, IPs, URLs, Hashes, and Cases. Discovers connected campaign clusters 
    sharing infrastructure across different attacks.
  - Incident Case Management: Allows analysts to create formal investigation 
    cases, assign severity, attach evidence, and log timestamped analyst notes.
  - Formal Forensic Report Generator: Creates printable HTML and PDF forensic 
    intelligence reports including executive summaries, technical matrices, 
    chain-of-custody ledgers, and legal disclaimers.


4. DATABASE ARCHITECTURE (22+ RELATIONAL TABLES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The relational database (PostgreSQL / SQLite) is organized into 5 core domains:

1. User & Access Control:
   - users, roles, permissions, audit_logs (tracks every analyst action).

2. Email & Forensic Parsing:
   - emails (master record with SHA-256, classification, scores).
   - email_headers (normalized RFC 5322 header key-value pairs).
   - received_hops (ordered relay hops, IPs, time deltas, TLS status).
   - authentication_results (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, ARC, lookalike trap flags).
   - urls (extracted links, anchor text, deceptive flags, entropy).
   - attachments (filenames, magic types, hashes, double-extension flags).

3. Threat Intelligence & Infrastructure:
   - indicators (IPs, domains, URLs, file hashes).
   - ip_enrichment (geo coordinates, country, city, ASN, ISP).
   - domain_enrichment (registration dates, DNS NS/MX records).
   - threat_intelligence_matches (AbuseIPDB, VirusTotal, URLhaus feed hits).
   - campaigns (discovered campaign clusters and actor profiles).

4. Cryptographic Evidence & Chain of Custody:
   - evidence_items (physical file path, immutable SHA-256 hash, legal hold).
   - custody_events (non-repudiable log of access, verification, and transfers).

5. Operations & Graph Topology:
   - cases (incident tickets, priority, status workflow).
   - analyst_notes (timestamped forensic observations).
   - alerts (SOC real-time alert notifications).
   - model_predictions (ML probabilities and feature token contributions).
   - graph_entities & graph_relationships (persisted network graph topology).


5. USER INTERFACE (15 DEDICATED ANALYST VIEWS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The frontend is built with React 18, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Lucide icons, 
and Leaflet GIS mapping:

 1. Overview Dashboard: Real-time SOC metrics, alert cards, recent ingestions.
 2. Ingest & Upload Email: Drag-and-drop .eml upload, raw paste tab, scenario picker.

 4. Headers & Relay Path: Interactive step-by-step visual hop timeline with time deltas.
 5. Auth & Alignment Matrix: Detailed SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and lookalike trap tables.
 6. URLs & Attachments: Extracted hyperlinks, deceptive anchors, and static file inspection.
 7. Geolocation & Infra Map: Leaflet world map with hop markers and ASN routing.
 8. Entity Correlation Graph: Visual network topology canvas with campaign clusters.
 9. Case Management: Incident tracker, status lifecycle, and investigation notes.
10. Evidence & Custody Log: One-click SHA-256 cryptographic integrity verifier.
11. Forensic Report Export: Live preview and printable HTML/PDF report export.
12. Threat Intelligence: Manual query tool for any IP, domain, URL, or hash.
13. User Directory & RBAC: Role-Based Access Control matrix for 4 distinct roles.
14. Immutable Audit Trail: Chronological security log of all analyst activities.
15. System Settings: Configurable scoring weights, threshold sliders, and disclaimers.
* Privacy Mode: One-click toggle in the top bar to mask recipient PII (e.g. j***e@org.com).


6. 5 SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION SCENARIOS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The platform includes 5 pre-packaged synthetic demonstration emails designed to 
prove detection across different modern attack vectors:

- Scenario A: Lookalike-Domain Phishing
  Spoofed as PayPal using typosquatted domain "paypa1-update.com" (homoglyph '1').
  Deceptive link displays "paypal.com" but points to an untrusted IP address.
  Result: Flagged for Lookalike Domain Trap, Deceptive Anchor, and Credential Harvest.

- Scenario B: Executive Impersonation (CEO Fraud)
  Display name matches CEO Dr. Arthur Vance, but sent from public @gmail.com.
  Demands emergency purchase of $2,500 in Apple gift cards for a private meeting.
  Result: Flagged for VIP display name on freemail + gift card/confidentiality cues.

- Scenario C: Business Email Compromise (BEC on Valid Domain)
  Sent from compromised legitimate partner domain "legit-partner.com".
  SPF and DKIM fully pass. Body requests updating bank routing for a $48,500 invoice.
  Result: Proves behavioral detection succeeds even when authentication passes.

- Scenario D: Malicious Double-Extension Attachment
  Fake court subpoena carrying attachment "Subpoena_Notice_Q3.pdf.exe".
  Inspects binary header to detect Windows PE ("MZ") executable magic bytes.
  Result: Flagged for Double Extension, Executable Payload, and Intimidation Language.

- Scenario E: Legitimate Corporate Communication
  Internal sprint review agenda from a legitimate enterprise domain.
  Full SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment, matching Reply-To, TLS encrypted hops.
  Result: Correctly classified as Benign / Clean (Score < 20/100).


7. DEFENSIVE CYBERSECURITY & ATTRIBUTION DISCLAIMER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A fundamental principle of defensive digital forensics is that technical signals 
alone CANNOT definitively determine the physical human identity of an attacker.

Because threat actors routinely route traffic through VPNs, Tor exit nodes, open 
relays, compromised cloud servers, or hijacked accounts, this platform adheres 
strictly to scientific, confidence-calibrated terminology:
  - "Probable sending infrastructure" (NOT "attacker's computer")
  - "Earliest reliable public IP" (NOT "hacker's home IP")
  - "Estimated geolocation" (NOT "guaranteed physical location")
  - "Confidence-based assessment" (NOT "absolute proof")
  - "Attribution support, not definitive attribution"

The platform is strictly for defensive cybersecurity, authorized investigation, 
education, and incident response.
